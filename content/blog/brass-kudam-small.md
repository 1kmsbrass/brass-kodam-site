# What Size Is a Small Brass Kudam?

“Small brass kudam” sounds simple.

Until three sellers show you three completely different products and all three call them small.

The lesson is straightforward:

**Small is not a measurement.**

Before buying, get the actual size.

## What should you check?

Ask for:

- Height
- Maximum diameter
- Opening diameter
- Capacity, if relevant
- Approximate weight
- Quantity

If you are buying online, ask for an actual photograph of the product as well.

A product photo helps you understand the shape and finish. Measurements tell you what you are actually getting.

## Capacity can matter more than appearance

For some buyers, the important question is not “Does it look small?”

It is:

**“Will it hold what I need?”**

If the kudam is being used for a particular pooja or ritual, confirm the required size or capacity with the priest or person organising the ceremony.

If it is for general household or decorative use, think about where you will keep it and how it will be handled.

A beautiful kudam that does not fit the job is still the wrong kudam.

## Does small mean cheap?

Not necessarily.

Price depends on more than the overall size. Weight, brass content, shape, workmanship, finishing and quantity can all affect the price.

So don't assume that two “small” kudams should have the same price.

## Buying several small kudams

If you are purchasing for a shop, temple, event or bulk requirement, give the quantity immediately.

For example:

> “I need 30 small brass kudams. Please send the available dimensions, approximate weight and bulk rate.”

That is a proper buying request.

It gives the supplier something concrete to work with.

## Buying one online

For one piece, ask the seller:

1. What is the height?
2. What is the diameter?
3. What is the approximate weight?
4. What is the capacity?
5. Is the displayed price inclusive of GST?
6. How is it packed for delivery?
7. What happens if it is damaged in transit?

Seven questions may sound like a lot.

But they are still cheaper than buying the wrong thing and then discovering it when the parcel arrives.

## The simple rule

Don't buy a **“small”** brass kudam.

Buy a brass kudam with **known dimensions and a suitable capacity**.

The word “small” can stay in the conversation. It just shouldn't be the specification.
