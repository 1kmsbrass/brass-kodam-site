# What Is the Brass Kudam Price?

If you search for **brass kudam price**, you will probably find a lot of numbers.

The problem is that a number without knowing the kudam is not very useful.

A brass kudam is not a packet of biscuits with one fixed MRP. Two kudams can look similar in a photograph and still have quite different weights, workmanship and prices.

So before asking, “What is the price?”, ask a slightly better question:

**“Which brass kudam, how many pieces, and what is the delivered price?”**

## What determines brass kudam price?

The main factors are:

- **Weight** — usually one of the biggest factors in a brass product.
- **Size and capacity** — a larger vessel may require more material.
- **Shape and workmanship** — handmade or more complicated forms can involve more labour.
- **Finish** — polishing and finishing affect the final product.
- **Quantity** — a single piece and a bulk order are different commercial conversations.
- **Packing and transport** — especially for heavier brass items.
- **GST and other charges** — check whether the quoted amount includes them.

This is why simply comparing two prices from two websites can be misleading.

## Why weight matters so much

Suppose two kudams are both described as “big size”.

That tells you almost nothing.

One may be lighter and thinner. Another may use considerably more brass. They may also differ in dimensions and capacity.

For a serious comparison, ask for:

1. Height
2. Maximum diameter
3. Opening diameter
4. Capacity, if relevant
5. Approximate weight
6. Quantity required

Now you are comparing products rather than adjectives.

## Should you buy by piece or by kg?

For a household buyer, a **piece price** is usually easier.

For a wholesale buyer, **weight and quantity** become much more important.

If someone gives you a price per kg, ask what exactly that rate represents. A finished kudam price is not necessarily just the raw metal rate multiplied by weight. Manufacturing, labour, wastage, finishing, packing, transport and the supplier's margin can all be part of the final price.

So compare the complete quotation, not just one number.

## What should you ask a supplier?

You can make the conversation very short:

> “I need ___ pieces of brass kudam, approximately ___ size, for delivery to ___. Please send the current rate and approximate weight per piece.”

That is much more useful than:

> “Brass kudam price?”

The second question forces the supplier to play detective.

## What about wholesale price?

If you are buying for a temple, pooja shop, function, dealer or other bulk requirement, tell the supplier the **quantity at the beginning**.

A quote for 2 pieces and a quote for 100 pieces are not necessarily the same commercial calculation.

Also mention the delivery location. A price that looks cheap at the factory can become less attractive after packing and transport.

## A sensible way to compare two quotes

Use this order:

**Product → dimensions → weight → quantity → piece price → GST → transport → delivered total**

That gives you a much cleaner comparison.

And if somebody gives you a suspiciously cheap price, don't immediately celebrate. First ask what is actually being supplied.

Brass is quite good at hiding differences inside something that looks shiny.
