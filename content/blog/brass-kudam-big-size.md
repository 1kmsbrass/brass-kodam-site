# How Do I Choose a Big Size Brass Kudam?

“Big size brass kudam.”

That sounds specific until you try to buy one.

How big is big?

This is where many buying conversations become unnecessarily complicated. **Big is a description, not a measurement.**

If you are buying a large brass kudam for a temple, Kumbabhishekam, pooja requirement or bulk order, get the actual dimensions and weight.

## What should “big size” mean?

At minimum, ask for:

- Height
- Maximum diameter
- Opening diameter
- Capacity, if relevant
- Approximate weight
- Number of pieces

A photograph can help you understand the shape. It cannot reliably tell you the weight.

And weight matters when you are comparing brass products.

## Bigger is not automatically better

A large kudam is useful only if it suits the job.

If it is being used for a ritual, the required size may be determined by the temple's or organisers' actual requirements. In that situation, don't choose a vessel simply because it looks impressive.

Ask the priest, temple authority or organiser what specification is required.

For general decorative or household use, capacity and handling may matter more.

## Why weight should be recorded

Imagine two large kudams sitting next to each other.

Both look substantial.

One weighs 4 kg and another weighs 6 kg.

They are not the same product just because both sellers call them “big”.

When requesting a quotation, ask:

**“What is the approximate weight per piece?”**

That one question can save a surprising amount of confusion.

## What about capacity?

Capacity is useful when the vessel has a practical job.

For example, if you need the kudam to hold a particular amount of water, saying “large” is much less useful than saying the approximate capacity you need.

For ritual requirements, however, follow the actual instructions given by the people conducting the ceremony. Ritual specifications can vary by temple and tradition.

## Buying a big brass kudam online

Before paying, ask the seller for:

- Actual product photographs
- Height
- Diameter
- Approximate weight
- Capacity where relevant
- Quantity available
- Packing method
- Delivery cost
- Replacement or damage policy

If the seller cannot tell you the basic dimensions of the product, you have learned something useful.

## For bulk buyers

If you need 20, 50 or 100 pieces, don't just say “big size”.

Send the requirement in a simple format:

**Product:** Brass kudam  
**Size:** ___  
**Quantity:** ___ pieces  
**Delivery:** ___ city

Now the supplier can quote something meaningful.

## The practical rule

When somebody says **“big brass kudam”**, your next question should be:

**“How big, exactly?”**

Not because we enjoy paperwork.

Because brass is sold in the real world, and the real world has measurements.
